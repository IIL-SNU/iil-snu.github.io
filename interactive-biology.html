Research/Interactive Biology

Interactive biology with living microorganisms

Seung Ah Lee and her laboratory developed interactive biology (human-biology interaction) systems in which a microscope and a projector let people interact in real time with living, light-responsive Euglena cells (S. A. Lee et al., Proceedings of the 33rd Annual ACM Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems 2015), including an immersive installation (K. Lee et al., Extended Abstracts of the 2020 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems 2020) and a review of the field of micro-HBI (S. A. Lee and I. H. Riedel-Kruse, Frontiers in Computer Science 2022). This is a past area of work.

Summary

Microscopic life is all around us, yet almost nobody gets to interact with it. Human-biology interaction (HBI) asks how people might play, create and learn with real living cells rather than simulations. Our systems couple a microscope to a projector: drawings on a touchscreen or hand gestures become patterns of light, and light-sensitive Euglena cells respond in real time. These platforms have become museum exhibits, immersive installations, biotic games and collaborative artworks, and they informed a review that maps the emerging field of micro-HBI. Kept here as a legacy theme, this work grew from the same expertise in optics and microscopy that drives our imaging research, and it continues to shape how we share science with the public.

Projects

5 projects

2015–2019

Trap it!: tangible interaction with living microorganisms

Trap it! lets anyone interact with real, living microorganisms. A touchscreen shows a live microscope view of Euglena; whatever a visitor draws is projected back into the microscope as a pattern of light, and the light-sensitive cells respond in real time, so they can be herded, corralled or trapped inside a drawn ring.

  • S. A. Lee et al., Proc. ACM CHI 2015, 2593–2602 (2015)
  • S. A. Lee et al., Proc. ACM TEI 2015, 273–280 (2015)
  • A. T. Lam et al., Nat. Biotechnol. 37, 1238 (2019)

2020

MicroAquarium: an immersive installation with living microorganisms

MicroAquarium turns a microscope into a room-sized aquarium of living cells. A custom light-projection microscope holds phototactic Euglena; a gesture sensor tracks visitors' hands, and their mid-air movements are converted into light patterns projected onto the cells.

  • K. Lee et al., CHI 2020 Extended Abstracts, 1–4 (2020)

2021

ReMember: recalling companion animals with biosignals

ReMember is an interactive installation for people who have lost a companion animal. It brings together two kinds of keepsake: the cremation stones that many owners keep, and recordings of their pet's heartbeat. The heartbeat is turned into sound and a pulsing ring of projected light whose color, brightness and size follow its rhythm.

  • C. Yi et al., Proc. ACM Comput. Graph. Interact. Tech. 4(2), 1–7 (2021), SIGGRAPH 2021

2022

EuglPollock: art making through interspecies collaboration

Can a microorganism be an artist's collaborator? EuglPollock, whose name blends Euglena with Jackson Pollock, is a platform where people and the alga Euglena gracilis make art together. Through a light-projection microscope, a participant's input becomes light that guides the swimming cells, and their movements are rendered as colorful, painterly trails on a large projection.

  • K. Lee et al., Proc. ACM Multimedia 2022, 1077–1084 (2022)

2020–2022

Micro-HBI and biotic games

Beyond individual installations, we helped define micro-HBI: interactive systems that give people real, first-hand experiences with living cells, viruses and molecules rather than simulations of them.
